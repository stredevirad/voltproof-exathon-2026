The problem

A consumer's memory against the utility's own reports

  • Daily power cuts and voltage too low or too high to use are common in parts of India.
  • Complaints rest on memory; there is rarely an independent, dated record.
  • Prayas's ESMI monitors recorded supply every minute at homes and shops, and published it, but as raw 63-column hourly files with gaps, duplicates and defects.

Illustrative A shopkeeper in a small Uttar Pradesh town loses business to evening cuts but cannot show when, how long, or whether neighbours lost supply too.

Who it helps

Evidence anyone can check, not a verdict

Consumers and consumer groups

A dated PDF per location-month: every interruption, its certainty and bounds, and a pointer to the grievance route.

Researchers and civil society

Reproducible, versioned SQL over the raw files; data defects found and documented instead of silently dropped.

Grievance forums and regulators

Every number traces to a source file line and an Exasol statement id, so a reviewer can verify it rather than trust it.

VoltProof is an evidence-analysis aid. It does not state what anyone is owed, does not name a cause, and is not legal advice.

How it works

The whole analysis is versioned SQL running inside Exasol

VerifyMD5 against Dataverse
→
IMPORTraw rows keep file id and line number
→
Unpivot63 columns → minutes; per-file date formats; quarantine
→
Runsvoltage classes; gaps-and-islands with one window function
→
Eventscertainty and bounds; daily, monthly, coverage
→
Coincidencepairs across monitors; chance baseline
  • Missing data is never an outage. Gaps stay NO_DATA; outages next to gaps get a measured lower bound (the headline) and an upper bound.
  • A month is comparable only with > 90% of minutes read (Prayas's rule).
  • FastAPI + React app and a one-click PDF report; every number traced to the source line and the Exasol statement id in the audit log.
  • Thresholds are Prayas's published ones: outage ≤ 130 V, low 131–204 V, high above 270 V.

Correctness evidence

The SQL must equal an independent implementation, exactly

  • 18 hand-written fixtures (rules R-1 to R-21), expected values derived by hand.
  • Independent plain-Python oracle: identical to the SQL on every fixture, on 40 random Hypothesis batches, and on real Uttar Pradesh slices (4,930 events; 20,127 coincident pairs).
  • Harness sensitivity: a single wrong value is caught.
  • Report consistency: every number in the PDF equals an independent SQL query and the rendered page.
  • Provenance: 199 of 199 statements matched to Exasol's audit log by exact SQL text.
  • End-to-end browser tests of the guided demo, plus a scan for blocked wording on every page and report.

VoltProof8

Data defects found in the published files

Found, documented and handled, never silently dropped

2019 Jan–June: all-0 V hours omitted lower bounds

In 2016–2018, 11–17% of read location-hours are entirely 0 V; in 2019, 4.3%. 82% of 2019 gaps have 0 V just before and after. Found by comparing with Prayas's own single-monitor charts.

Handled: gaps stay missing data; 2019 figures are labelled lower bounds in the app and reports; headlines use 2016–2018.

2019 July–Dec: Hour column lost

690,915 of 695,974 rows say hour 0, so minute timestamps cannot be determined. Loaded, verified, excluded with that reason.

2019 formats

Dates M/D/YYYY (earlier DD-MM-YYYY), detected per file; names with an added " [Offline]", matched only when unambiguous.

Limits and legal framing

What VoltProof does not claim

  • A plug-in monitor is not the utility's meter; an outage may also come from a tripped breaker, wiring or the monitor.
  • ESMI data is historical (2014–2019): it describes supply then, not today.
  • A potential shared supply event is a coincidence in time, not proof of a common cause.
  • No amount owed and no breach is stated. Not legal advice.

Grievance route the report points to

In Uttar Pradesh, the Commission's regulations under Section 42(5)–(8) of the Electricity Act, 2003 provide a Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum (s. 42(5)) and an Electricity Ombudsman (s. 42(6)).

UPERC CGRF and Ombudsman Regulations, 2003; PIB, Ministry of Power, 30 Dec 2010. The Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, 2020 (PIB, 21 Dec 2020) postdate the data and are not applied to it.
