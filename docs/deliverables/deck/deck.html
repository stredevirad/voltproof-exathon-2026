VoltProof

Traceable evidence of power cuts and poor supply, from minute-by-minute voltage records, analysed inside Exasol.

Households and small shops on unreliable electricity can't show when, and for how long, their power failed, because when they complain, their own memory is usually all they have to set against the utility's records. If this worked, they could take a dated, minute-by-minute record of every outage and voltage problem, with its uncertainty stated, to their utility or grievance forum.

The problem

Evening power cuts, and nothing to show for them

Illustrative

“The power goes off almost every evening.”

A shopkeeper's memory, against the utility's own records.

A shopkeeper in a small Uttar Pradesh town loses business to evening cuts. When he complains, he cannot show when the power failed, for how long, or whether his neighbours lost it too.

Daily cuts and voltage too low to use are common in parts of India, and complaints rarely have an independent, dated record behind them.

The record already exists

Independent monitors recorded the voltage every minute

  • Prayas (Energy Group)'s ESMI monitors were plugged in at homes and shops and recorded the voltage every minute. VoltProof uses Uttar Pradesh, January 2016 to June 2019.
  • The readings are published openly, but as raw hourly files with gaps, duplicates and defects: hard to use, and impossible to show to a utility as they are.

What VoltProof gives him

Every cut, dated to the minute, with its certainty stated

  • One monitor, one month: at least 366.2 hours without supply, every cut listed.
  • Missing data is never counted, so the headline is a minimum.
  • Open any day to see each minute; open any cut to see its source lines.
  • One click: a PDF evidence report to take to the utility.
  • Anyone can analyse readings from their own monitor today, the same way; the app labels them “not verified”.

Demo moment · Bahraich, 25 July 2018, 18:27

One evening in Bahraich: 17 monitors lost supply within 2 minutes

  • Huzurpur-Bahraich plus 16 other Bahraich monitors lost supply between 18:27 and 18:29 (the panel lists the 16 others).
  • VoltProof calls this a potential shared supply event: the cuts started together. It names no cause and no equipment.
  • Chosen by a fixed, documented rule, not hand-picked: the evening potential shared supply event with the most monitors in June–August 2018.

The whole month, not just this evening: in Bahraich in July 2018, interruptions at two different monitors started within 2 minutes of each other 3,945 times; about 1,827.5 would be expected by chance (20 versions with the days shifted; none reached the observed count).

Coincidence is not cause.

How it works, in plain words

Four steps, from published files to a report

1
Check

Every file's checksum (a fingerprint of its contents) must match the published one before it is used.

2
Load

The raw rows go into the database unchanged, each one keeping its file and line number.

3
Analyse

Every minute is sorted into no supply, low, normal, high or no reading; each cut is found, measured and compared with other monitors.

4
Show

The app and the PDF only read the results. Every number points back to its file line.

Steps 2 and 3 run inside Exasol (next slide).

Missing data is never an outage. A cut next to a gap gets a measured minimum (the headline) and a possible maximum.

The voltage classes are Prayas's published ones: no supply at 130 V or less, low 131–204 V, high above 270 V. A month is compared only when more than 90% of its minutes were read.

Honest about the data

Defects in the published files: found and labelled, never hidden

2019 January–June lower bounds

Hours that read 0 V throughout were left out of this file. The gaps stay missing data, so 2019 figures are labelled lower bounds in the app and the reports.

2019 July–December: Hour column lost

Almost every row says hour 0, so the minute of a reading cannot be known. The file is loaded, checked and excluded, with that reason.

2019 names and dates

Some locations gained an added “ [Offline]”; they are matched to their earlier names only when that is unambiguous. Dates changed format between files and are read per file.

Limits and legal framing

What VoltProof does not claim

  • A plug-in monitor is not the utility's meter; an outage may also come from a tripped breaker, wiring or the monitor.
  • ESMI data is historical (the whole dataset: 2014–2019; used here: Uttar Pradesh, January 2016 to June 2019): it describes supply then, not today.
  • A potential shared supply event is a coincidence in time, not proof of a common cause.
  • No amount owed and no breach is stated. VoltProof is an evidence-analysis aid, not legal advice.

Grievance route the report points to

In Uttar Pradesh, the Commission's 2003 regulations provide a Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum and an Electricity Ombudsman.

UPERC CGRF and Ombudsman Regulations, 2003; PIB, Ministry of Power, 30 Dec 2010.

Who could use it next

Who could use it, and what comes next

Households and small shops

A dated record of every cut, with its certainty, instead of a memory, to take to their utility or grievance forum. Anyone can also upload readings from their own monitor today; the app labels them “not verified”.

Consumer groups, researchers, journalists

Event-level evidence and a report per location-month, instead of raw minute files. Today that means the ESMI records.

A grievance forum or a regulator

Each number traces to a file line and an Exasol statement, so it can be checked rather than trusted.

Next (not claimed today): New monitors or smart-meter data through the same SQL; each state's standards of performance, from the primary regulation, to show which events may warrant review through the grievance forum.

More states and maps: the ESMI files cover many states; a map needs coordinates the source does not have. For a household without a monitor, VoltProof is a method today, not a service.

Appendix A4 · Data defects found in the published files

Found, documented and handled, never silently dropped

2019 Jan–June: all-0 V hours omitted lower bounds

In 2016–2018, 11–17% of read location-hours are entirely 0 V; in 2019, 4.3%. 82% of 2019 gaps have 0 V just before and after. Found by comparing with Prayas's own single-monitor charts.

Handled: gaps stay missing data; 2019 figures are labelled lower bounds in the app and reports; headlines use 2016–2018.

2019 July–Dec: Hour column lost

690,915 of 695,974 rows say hour 0, so minute timestamps cannot be determined. Loaded, verified, excluded with that reason.

2019 formats

Dates M/D/YYYY (earlier DD-MM-YYYY), detected per file; names with an added " [Offline]", matched only when unambiguous.
